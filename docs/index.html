      Dollar ledger

      A plain dollar account, kept separately from the tonnes above. Overall prices every gram emitted at the removal rate.       Audit trail — offsets and donations

      Everything a tax year needs, in one place: who paid, what was bought, what it cost, and the proof. Each purchase carries the SHA-256 of its receipt as recorded at filing time, the registry retirement ID where one was issued, and the receipt itself as a download built from the stored bytes — no server, no link to rot. Two payers keep separate books: Signalblur Security and Lies Above Media.

      For an accountant-ready CSV, one file per payer with a totals row, run scripts/offset-export.sh --tax-year YYYY (add --payer NAME to narrow it). The export records; it does not give tax advice — classify donation-versus-expense with your CPA.

      Where to give

      Seven destinations, one per ecosystem. The first two act on the balance above — removal settles it, prevention complements it without settling. The other five are restoration and land conservation, and the carbon verdict on several of them is honestly unfavourable; it is printed here rather than smoothed over. Every claim links to its evidence, and every org links to wherever it publishes news.

      • Air — removal

        Remove Carbon Today — biochar CORCs

        Ex-post biochar carbon-removal certificates, independently weighed and sampled, audited under the Puro Standard and retired in your name on the public registry. About $160 per tonne; stock rotates and sells out.

        The only pathway here that settles the balance above: verified removal, already done.

        • Sources retirement proof, Nasdaq CORCCHAR index
        • News Puro.earth insights (the registry — the vendor publishes no newsroom)
      • Air — prevention

        Tradewater

        Collects and destroys refrigerants and plugs orphaned methane wells. Every container is third-party weighed, sampled and lab-analysed, certified under ACR or VCS. Around $15 per tonne, which is well under comparable refrigerant-destruction pricing — worth a direct quote before a large purchase.

        Avoidance, not removal. It stops a future emission; it does not take carbon back out, so it never settles the balance.

        • Sources VCS verification report, TW Ghana ODS (GHD Ltd, 2019)
        • News Tradewater press
      • Rivers

        American Rivers

        Dam removal and river restoration. Reservoir surfaces are a large methane source, and the four removed Klamath River dams are estimated to have eliminated roughly 275,000 tonnes CO2e a year.

        The methane avoidance is real and reasonably well quantified for specific dams, but a donation cannot be converted into a tonnage: American Rivers is one partner among many funders and sells no credit.

        • Sources CARB-hosted Klamath methane estimate (2026), Deemer et al. 2016, BioScience
        • News Media center
      • Oceans

        Coral Restoration Foundation

        Propagates and outplants staghorn coral in the Florida Keys. Dense outplanting has been shown to return net-erosional reefs to positive carbonate accretion — that is reef structure rebuilt.

        Habitat restoration, not a carbon investment. Coral calcification releases CO2, and no published CRF carbon claim exists to check. Fund it for the reef, the coastline and the biodiversity.

        • Sources Esplandiu et al. 2024, Scientific Reports — reef carbonate budgets
        • News CRF blog
      • Coasts & estuaries

        Billion Oyster Project

        Self-sustaining oyster reefs in New York Harbor, with 150 million-plus oysters and 17 acres built. Filtration, storm-surge buffering, habitat, and a schools programme attached to all of it.

        Not a carbon investment, and possibly a net source: on exposed sandflats — much of this footprint — inorganic carbon burial vents more CO2 than the organic burial takes down. Fund it for the harbour.

        • Sources Fodrie et al. 2017, Proc. R. Soc. B — oyster reefs as carbon sources and sinks
        • News BOP stories
      • Land & watershed

        Naturaland Trust

        Buys and protects Blue Ridge escarpment land above Greenville — including 1,090 acres at Saluda Bluffs — in the watershed that feeds Greenville's own drinking-water reservoirs.

        The only non-market org here where a rough number can be built from public data: about $20–150 per tonne depending on method, which is a five-to-eightfold spread and no registry stands behind it.

        • Sources Greenville Journal — Saluda Bluffs, 1,090 acres
        • News Newsletter & archive (no newsroom published)
      • Wetlands & swamp

        Congaree Land Trust

        Holds easements across the COWASEE basin around Congaree National Park — the largest old-growth bottomland hardwood forest in the eastern United States, and one of the densest soil-carbon stores of any protected forest type in North America.

        No defensible price per tonne, because the easements are largely donated: there is no transaction to divide the carbon stock by. Fund it for the forest, and say so plainly.

        • Sources Sapkota et al. 2025, Frontiers — freshwater forested wetland soil carbon
        • News CLT news

      How these numbers are made

      Every figure on this page is derived from stored token counts by a published factor. Nothing is measured. Anthropic publishes no per-query energy data, so the honest description of all of it is order-of-magnitude, roughly ±50%. Trends and relative comparisons are meaningful; absolute values are indicative.

      Fuller derivations, the uncertainty bands per model family, and the cross-check gate against an independent estimator are in METHODOLOGY.md.

      Issued Recomputed from stored token counts. No figure here is a measurement.
